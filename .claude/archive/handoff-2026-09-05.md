## 2. Teaching calibration (not in the project instructions)

- When a technique is _new_ (not a variation), he will ask for the worked example
  directly; holding out for a prose algorithm first stalled him (2026-07-19). Reserve
  "describe it in words first" for variations on something he has already done.
- Abstract advice ("mount as a plugin") got "talk me through this in more detail";
  what landed was reading his files and pointing at code he had already written
  (2026-08-07). Ground in his repo before explaining a mechanism.
- Nudge tolerance is roughly one: after a nudge fails he says so plainly ("I honestly
  don't know…", "I'm not sure what you mean by…"). Treat that as the cue to drop to
  mechanics/sandbox, not to rephrase the question.
- He pushed back twice on camelCase discriminant values; what worked was holding the
  position while honestly downgrading confidence and telling him to decide once and
  record it (2026-07-18, `(summary)`). Don't cave, don't restate.
- He rejects heuristics that infer provenance from format (`.length(5)`) as brittle
  (2026-07-25). Offer the honest option, not the clever one.
- He caught me on unverified claims four times (tsc exhaustiveness, Node error codes,
  `instanceof DatabaseError`, `category: "customer"`). He expects file reads or an
  empirical test before an assertion, and notices when I skip that.
- The `/ready` neverthrow exercise "felt like ceremony" (2026-07-18, `(summary)`). He
  tolerates a concept's payoff being deferred if that's said upfront, not discovered.
- Worklist-first review format worked well (2026-08-12) — he replied "check 2" to
  advance items, so numbered items are how he navigates a review.
- Formats that worked: three-row result tables mapping query outcome → meaning
  (2026-08-07); indented tree diagrams for plugin scope; "run the three cases in your
  head" as the closing question after a sandbox.
